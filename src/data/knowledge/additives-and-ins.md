# FSSAI — Food Additives and INS Numbers

## What is a food additive

A food additive is any substance not normally consumed as food by itself, not normally used as a
typical ingredient of food, and intentionally added to food for a technological purpose such as
preservation, colouring, flavouring, sweetening, thickening or emulsification. Additives are not
used to disguise damaged or substandard food or to deceive the consumer.

## INS number system

INS (International Numbering System) numbers are the internationally agreed identifiers assigned
to food additives. The same additive may have one INS number under which it is referenced on
Indian labels. INS 100 series are colours, 200 series preservatives, 300 series antioxidants and
acidity regulators, 400 series emulsifiers and stabilisers, 500 series acidity regulators and
anti-caking agents, 600 series flavour enhancers, and 900 series sweeteners and other substances.

## Common additives and their uses

- INS 102 (Tartrazine), INS 110 (Sunset Yellow FCF), INS 122 (Carmoisine), INS 129 (Allura Red),
  INS 133 (Brilliant Blue) — synthetic colours.
- INS 211 (Sodium benzoate), INS 202 (Potassium sorbate) — preservatives.
- INS 330 (Citric acid), INS 331 (Sodium citrates) — acidity regulators.
- INS 621 (Monosodium glutamate / MSG) — flavour enhancer.
- INS 415 (Xanthan gum), INS 412 (Guar gum) — stabilisers/thickeners.
- INS 322 (Lecithin) — emulsifier.
- INS 951 (Aspartame), INS 955 (Sucralose), INS 960 (Steviol glycosides) — sweeteners.

## Permitted use

An additive may be used only if it is listed as permitted for that food category in the Food
Standards (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations, 2011, and only within the
specified maximum permitted level (MPL). Additives must be safe for the purpose, serve a genuine
technological need, and their use must not mislead the consumer. Foods containing additives beyond
permitted levels are sub-standard under the Act.

## Labelling of additives

Additives in the ingredient list must be declared by their category name and INS number (e.g.,
"Colour (INS 102)"). When a flavour is declared, it must be stated whether it is natural,
nature-identical, or artificial (e.g., "Natural flavouring substances", "Artificial flavouring
substances").

## Sulphites and other allergens

Sulphites in concentrations of 10 ppm or more must be declared. Additives that are known allergens
must be labelled prominently so consumers with sensitivities can avoid them.
