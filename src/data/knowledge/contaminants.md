# FSSAI — Contaminants, Toxins and Residues

## Contaminants regulated

The Food Safety and Standards (Contaminants, Toxins and Residues) Regulations, 2011 prescribe
maximum permissible limits for:

- Heavy metals and metallic contaminants: lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, tin, chromium and
  copper.
- Aflatoxins and mycotoxins: aflatoxin B1, total aflatoxins (B1+B2+G1+G2), ochratoxin A.
- Pesticide residues: limits consistent with the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act regime and
  Codex maximum residue limits (MRLs).
- Antibiotic residues and veterinary drug residues in foods of animal origin.
- Naturally occurring toxic substances (e.g., hydrocyanic acid in certain beans, erucic acid in
  oils).

## Example limits

Common maximum limits include: lead not exceeding 2.5 ppm in most foods (lower for infant food),
aflatoxin B1 not exceeding 30 micrograms per kg in cereals, and aflatoxin total not exceeding 15
micrograms per kg in certain nuts and oilseeds. Limits vary by food category, so the specific
product table in the regulations must be consulted.

## Sampling and analysis

Compliance is assessed by Food Safety Officers who draw samples under the Act and send them to
FSSAI-notified laboratories. Analysis follows the prescribed methods. A product is deemed unsafe
if it contains a contaminant above the permissible limit, which may lead to prosecution of the
manufacturer.

## Radionuclides

Limits are also specified for radioactive contamination following emergencies, aligned with the
International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) guidance.

## Consequences of exceeding limits

Food exceeding contaminant limits is treated as unsafe food under Section 3(1)(zz) of the FSS Act
and is liable to be seized, with the operator facing penalties under Sections 59-61 of the Act,
including fines and imprisonment depending on whether the contamination endangers life.
