# FSSAI — The Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 (Overview)

## What the Act does

The Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 (FSS Act, 2006) established the Food Safety and Standards
Authority of India (FSSAI) as a single statutory authority for all matters relating to food safety
and standards in India. It consolidates all previous food laws, including the Prevention of Food
Adulteration Act, 1954, the Fruit Products Order, 1955, the Meat Food Products Order, 1973, the
Vegetable Oil Products (Control) Order, 1947, the Edible Oils Packaging (Regulation) Order, 1988,
and the Solvent Extracted Oil, De-Oiled Meal and Edible Flour (Control) Order, 1967.

## Key objectives

The Act's primary objectives are to regulate the manufacture, storage, distribution, sale and
import of food so as to ensure safe and wholesome food, protect consumers from unsafe, adulterated
or misbranded food, and lay down science-based standards for articles of food.

## Key definitions (Section 3)

- "Food" means any substance, whether processed, semi-processed or raw, intended for human
  consumption and includes primary food, genetically modified or engineered food, infant food,
  packaged drinking water, alcoholic drinks, chewing gum, and any substance used as an ingredient
  in the preparation of food.
- "Adulterant" means any material which is or could be employed for making the food unsafe or
  sub-standard, misbranded or containing extraneous matter.
- "Unsafe food" means food whose nature, substance or quality is affected so as to render it
  injurious to health by reason of contamination, adulteration, impurity, putrefaction, incorrect
  processing, misbranding or false declaration.
- "Manufacturer", "distributor" and "retailer" are defined for the purposes of responsibility for
  food safety along the supply chain.

## Central licensing vs state licensing

FSSAI issues central licenses for large food business operators (FBOs) including importers,
manufacturers whose production capacity exceeds prescribed limits, and businesses operating in
multiple states. State authorities issue state licenses and registrations for smaller operators.
Every FBO is required to hold a valid FSSAI license or registration number that must appear on the
product label.

## Offences and penalties

The Act prescribes graded penalties: from fines for sub-standard and misbranded food, to
imprisonment for unsafe food, adulteration causing injury or death, and the manufacture or sale of
food containing extraneous matter. Repeat offences attract higher penalties and mandatory license
cancellation. Consumers may claim compensation for injury caused by unsafe food.

## Enforcement bodies

Enforcement is carried out by the Food Safety Officer (FSO) at the local level, the Commissioner of
Food Safety at the state level, and the Chief Executive Officer of FSSAI at the national level.
Adjudicating Officers and the Food Safety Appellate Tribunal hear appeals.
