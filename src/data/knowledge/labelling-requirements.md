# FSSAI — Packaged Food Labelling Requirements

## General labelling requirements (FSS (Labelling and Display) Regulations, 2020)

Every pre-packaged food offered for sale must bear a label that is legible, prominent, and
conspicuous. The label must be in English and Hindi (or English and a regional language) and must
contain all of the following information:

1. The name of the food (product name that does not mislead the consumer about its true nature).
2. A complete list of ingredients in descending order of their composition by weight.
3. Nutritional information (nutrition facts panel).
4. The name and address of the manufacturer, and the FSSAI license number.
5. The net quantity of the product.
6. The "Best Before" or "Use By" date, and the manufacturing date where required.
7. The Lot/Code number for traceability.
8. Instructions for storage and use where applicable.

## Ingredient list rules

Ingredients must be declared in descending order of weight at the time of manufacture. The list
must use the specific common or usual name of each ingredient; generic names such as "flavour" or
"spices" may only be used when the specific name is not required by the regulations. Food additives
must be declared by category name followed by the INS number or the additive's name (e.g., "Acidity
Regulator (INS 330)").

## Nutrition facts panel

The panel must declare energy (kcal), total carbohydrate, sugars, added sugar, protein, total fat,
saturated fat, trans fat, cholesterol, sodium, and total dietary fibre. Values are expressed per
100 g / 100 ml, with a declaration of the serving size where the label declares per-serving
values. Health or nutrient claims must not be made unless the product meets the conditions for the
claim under the regulations.

## Date marking

"Best Before" indicates the date up to which the food retains its optimum quality. "Use By" is
used for perishable food and indicates the last date on which the food may be safely consumed.
Products past their "Use By" date must not be sold.

## Language and legibility

Information must be legible, in a font size not less than 1.5 mm, and must not be obscured,
pinned, or written in a way that is hidden by any other written, printed or graphic matter.

## Exemptions and special categories

Certain products are exempt from full labelling (e.g., food sold loose or in open containers in
retail premises, provided safety information is displayed nearby). Infant formula, foods for
special medical purposes, and health supplements are regulated by separate, stricter labelling
regimes.
