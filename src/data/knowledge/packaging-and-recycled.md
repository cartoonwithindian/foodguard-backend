# FSSAI — Packaging, Recycled Plastics and Recyclability Mark

## General packaging requirements

Packaging materials must not contaminate the food or impart toxic, tainted or deteriorating
substances to it. Materials in contact with food must be food-grade and comply with the standards
prescribed under the FSSAI regulations. Packaging must not mislead the consumer about the content,
weight or quality of the product.

## Recycled plastics in food contact

The Food Safety and Standards (Packaging) Regulations, 2018 govern the use of recycled plastics
for food contact. Recycled plastics may be used only after approval by the Food Authority, based
on a scientific assessment of the material's suitability, unless the recycling process is
recognised as safe internationally. Pre-consumer (post-industrial) waste is generally acceptable,
while post-consumer recycled plastic requires case-by-case approval to ensure it is free of
contaminants and fit for food contact.

## The recyclability mark

The FSSAI "Recycled" logo must be printed on all plastic packaging (including multi-layered
plastic packaging) manufactured after 1 July 2022, to indicate that the packaging is recyclable.
The mark includes the words "Recycled" and the recycling symbol. Non-compliance is punishable with
a penalty under the Act. The mark requirement was introduced to promote the collection and
recycling of plastic packaging.

## Multi-layered plastics

Multi-layered plastic packaging requires the recyclability mark as well, and manufacturers must
comply with extended producer responsibility (EPR) obligations under the Plastic Waste Management
Rules administered by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC).

## Glass, metal and paper

Glass containers must be clean and free from defects that could contaminate the food. Cans must
have a safe internal lacquer to prevent metal migration. Paper and board must not impart taint,
odour or toxic substances and must be suitable for the food type and storage conditions.
